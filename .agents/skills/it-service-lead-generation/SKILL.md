# IT service lead generation

Connect a specific buyer problem to an IT service the business can actually deliver. Identify the offered service, target industry, company size, buyer role, geography, buying trigger, and available proof from project context. Separate confirmed business facts from proposed positioning.

## Build a focused acquisition flow

1. Define an ideal customer profile and exclusions. Distinguish company fit from current buying intent; a relevant company is not automatically a qualified opportunity.
2. Match one offer to one primary intent. Examples include a modernization assessment, cloud-cost review, or scoped product discovery. Explain deliverables, eligibility, and the next step without promising unverified outcomes.
3. Choose channels based on that intent and the available budget: service-specific search pages for active demand, useful technical content for research-stage buyers, or account-specific outreach drafts for named prospects.
4. Align the message, landing-page promise, form, and follow-up. Draft copy that names the buyer problem, credible evidence, offer, and a clear action.

## Capture and qualification

Collect only fields that change routing or qualification. A starting form can use name, business email, company, and project need, with budget and timing optional unless operationally necessary. Define validation, submission progress, duplicate handling, failure recovery, and a truthful confirmation. Separate newsletter opt-in from an inquiry.

Use an explicit, adjustable qualification rubric: service fit, account fit, urgency, and access to the buying decision. Keep unknown values unknown. Document why each lead receives its status and which next action follows; do not invent contact information or infer buying intent from a job title alone.

When researching prospects, keep the source URL, observation date, organization, relevant public business signal, and confidence. Label hypotheses. Drafting a campaign does not authorize sending messages, buying lists, enrolling contacts, or uploading prospect data to external systems.

## Measurement and handoff

Define stages such as visit, inquiry, qualified inquiry, meeting, opportunity, and customer. Specify each transition and denominator before reporting conversion rates. Track successful form submissions rather than button clicks as inquiries; avoid personal data in analytics events or URLs.

For a strategy request, deliver the audience definition, offer, channel rationale, sample copy, qualification rubric, and a prioritized experiment. For implementation, use the actual backend contract and expose failures; never simulate successful delivery in a production flow. Report measured results separately from forecasts.
